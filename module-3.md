# Module 3 – Hazards, Occupational Exposure, Waste, Spills, Incident Reporting and Basic Reprocessing

## 3.1 Hazard Identification and Infection Risk Management
For the purposes of this guide, infection-related hazards can be considered across four broad categories:
* **Biological Hazards:** Pathogenic microorganisms in blood, saliva, tissue, or DUWL biofilm.
* **Chemical Hazards:** Hazardous chemicals used for surface cleaning, disinfection, or reprocessing.
* **Physical Hazards:** Contaminated sharp items including burs, needles, scalpels, and matrix bands.
* **Environmental Hazards:** Inadequate ventilation, damaged barriers, or overflowing waste receptacles.

Risks are assessed based on likelihood and severity, applying the 6-level Hierarchy of Controls. Hazards that cannot be immediately rectified are logged and reported to supervisors.

## 3.2 Occupational Exposure and First Aid
An occupational exposure occurs when a healthcare worker comes into contact with potentially infectious blood or body substances during their work duties, through broken skin, mucous membranes or a percutaneous injury.

> **IMMEDIATE EXPOSURE FIRST AID RESPONSE**
> 1. **PERCUTANEOUS INJURY:** Wash gently with soap and warm water. Do NOT scrub, squeeze or deliberately force bleeding. Apply waterproof dressing.
> 2. **MUCOUS MEMBRANE SPLASH:** Flush immediately with large volumes of clean water or sterile saline.
> 3. **NON-INTACT SKIN EXPOSURE:** Wash thoroughly with warm water and soap without scrubbing.
> 4. **NOTIFY & REFER:** Inform supervisor immediately; attend medical evaluation promptly. Evaluating medical professional determines all investigations and treatment.

## 3.3 Sharps Safety
Contaminated sharps are an important physical and biological hazard in dental practice. Sharps include local anaesthetic needles and delivery components, burs, endodontic files, scalpels, and matrix bands. Sharps must remain visible and under control. Manual two-handed needle recapping is prohibited. Sharps are disposed of immediately after use at point-of-use into approved sharps containers complying with Australian requirements.

## 3.4 Clinical Waste Management
Dental waste streams include General Waste (standard trash bin), Clinical/Biohazard Waste (items visibly saturated/dripping with blood, segregated at point of generation into designated biohazard receptacles), Sharps Waste (rigid puncture-resistant sharps container), and Pharmaceutical/Chemical Waste. Exact waste definitions and containers follow state/territory legislation and workplace policy.

## 3.5 Spills and Contamination Events
**Spill Response Sequence:** Isolate area -> Don appropriate PPE -> Cover and absorb liquid spill with disposable towels -> Wipe area with water and neutral detergent to remove visible soil -> Apply appropriate hospital-grade disinfectant per manufacturer instructions -> Segregate waste and perform hand hygiene.

Mechanical tools (dustpan/scraper, forceps) must be used to pick up broken glass or sharp spill debris. Where disinfection is required, visible contamination should be removed by cleaning before applying disinfectant.

## 3.6 Incident Reporting, Documentation, and Escalation
IPC incidents (exposures, equipment breaches, spills) must be reported promptly to designated supervisors. Incident records must contain objective, factual narrative details (date, time, location, persons involved, actions taken) without assigning blame, maintaining secure record confidentiality.

## 3.7 Clean, Disinfected, and Sterile Storage
Processed reusable instruments are stored in clean, dry, dust-free cabinets away from moisture and direct sunlight. Package integrity checks before chairside use include verifying seal integrity, absence of moisture, chemical indicator changes, and storage details per workplace procedure. If packaging is compromised, the item must not be used and must be reprocessed.

## 3.8 Basic Dental Instrument Reprocessing Workflow
Reprocessing ensures reusable medical devices are safe for patient care, guided by the Spaulding Classification Framework:
* **Critical Items:** Items entering sterile tissue or vascular system (burs, forceps, scalers). Required level: Sterilisation.
* **Semi-Critical Items:** Items contacting intact mucous membranes (mirrors, restorative instruments, matrix retainers). Required level: Appropriate reprocessing according to intended use, manufacturer instructions, and IPC guidance. Reusable dental instruments that can be sterilised should be sterilised.
* **Non-Critical Items:** Items contacting intact skin only (protective eyewear, bib chains). Required level: Cleaning followed by appropriate disinfection where indicated.

> **8-STEP BASIC DENTAL REPROCESSING CYCLE**
> 1. **POINT-OF-USE CARE:** Wipe heavy organic soil chairside; keep moist.
> 2. **SAFE TRANSPORT:** Transport in rigid, puncture-resistant, leak-proof, covered container.
> 3. **CLEANING:** Automated washer-disinfector, ultrasonic cleaner, or manual washing according to instructions.
> 4. **RINSING & DRYING:** Rinse detergent residues; dry completely before inspection/packaging.
> 5. **INSPECTION:** Inspect under good lighting for cleanliness, bioburden, and damage.
> 6. **PACKAGING:** Package items requiring sterilisation in approved packaging with process indicators.
> 7. **STERILISATION:** Process instruments in a steam steriliser according to the manufacturer's instructions, workplace procedure and applicable infection prevention and control requirements.
> 8. **STORAGE & TRACKING:** Store in clean dry environment; record load details per workplace procedure.

## 3.9 Reprocessing Safety and Quality Controls
Reprocessing safety requires heavy-duty utility gloves, protective eyewear, mask, and fluid-resistant apron. Zoning maintains strict separation between Contaminated receiving/cleaning areas and Clean packaging/sterilisation areas. Quality checks include visual inspection, checking process indicators, verifying steriliser cycle physical readings, reporting cycle faults, and completing logbook traceability entries.

## 3.10 Applying IPC in the Dental Workplace: Clinical Appointment Workflow
IPC principles integrate across a typical appointment: Phase 1 (Pre-appointment setup, barrier placement, waterline/suction checks, zoning), Phase 2 (Chairside care, PPE donning, HVE aerosol control, sharps control), Phase 3 (Post-treatment cleanup, sharps disposal, waste segregation, surface decontamination), and Phase 4 (Instrument reprocessing, cycle logging, utility PPE, hand hygiene).
