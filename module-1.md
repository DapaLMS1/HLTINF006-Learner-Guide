# Module 1 – Infection Prevention and Control Foundations

## 1.1 Purpose of Infection Prevention and Control in Dental Practice
Infection prevention and control (IPC) is essential to clinical safety in every Australian dental practice. Depending on the specific procedure being performed, dental care can involve exposure to saliva, blood, respiratory secretions, contaminated clinical instruments, and contaminated environmental surfaces. Dental procedures may also involve droplet and aerosol generation from equipment such as high-speed handpieces, air-water syringes, and ultrasonic scalers. Without structured infection control protocols, pathogenic microorganisms can be transmitted between patients, registered dental practitioners, and clinical support staff.

The primary purpose of IPC in dental practice is to:
* **Protect Patients:** Minimise the risk of healthcare-associated infections (HAIs) during invasive and non-invasive dental procedures.
* **Protect the Dental Team:** Safeguard dental assistants, dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists, dental prosthetists, and administrative staff from occupational exposure to blood-borne viruses and respiratory pathogens.
* **Maintain Environmental Safety:** Prevent contamination of physical clinical zones, dental unit waterlines, instruments, and dental equipment.
* **Fulfil Professional and Workplace Standards:** Support workplace health and safety compliance and maintain professional standards across the practice.

## 1.2 Governance, Professional Frameworks, and Responsibilities
Infection prevention and control in Australian dental practice operates within a framework of legislation, professional regulation, standards, and clinical guidance. Understanding how these elements differ helps clarify roles and responsibilities in the workplace.

* **Work Health and Safety (WHS) Legislation:** State and territory WHS laws create legal duties for employers (referred to as a Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking, or PCBU) and workers. Employers must provide a safe working environment, safe systems of work, and appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE). Workers—including dental assistants—have a legal obligation to take reasonable care for their own health and safety, follow reasonable safety instructions, and cooperate with workplace health and safety policies and procedures.
* **Dental Board of Australia (DBA):** The DBA regulates registered dental practitioners under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law. Registered practitioners must comply with the DBA Infection prevention and control guidelines to maintain registration. While dental assistants are not individually registered with the DBA, they work under supervision and must follow practice policies aligning with these regulatory standards.
* **Australian Standards (AS 5369:2023):** Reprocessing of reusable medical devices and other devices in health and non-health service organisations provides requirements and guidance for the safe reprocessing of reusable medical devices, setting technical benchmarks for cleaning, inspection, packaging, sterilisation, and storage.
* **Clinical Practice Guidelines (ADA & NHMRC):** The ADA Infection Control Guidelines translate national standards and evidence into practical, dental-specific workflows. Overarching national guidelines from the NHMRC provide evidence-based guidance for standard and transmission-based precautions across healthcare settings.

## 1.3 Dental Assistant Responsibilities & Workplace Accountability
As a dental assistant, you play a direct role in applying infection prevention and control procedures in the dental practice. Your responsibilities are performed according to your workplace policies and procedures, your training, your defined job role, relevant regulatory requirements, and manufacturer instructions.

Clinical support responsibilities include:
* **Applying Standard Precautions:** Consistently carrying out hand hygiene, wearing appropriate PPE, performing environmental surface cleaning, and handling sharps safely for every patient.
* **Maintaining Clinical Zoning:** Setting up and maintaining clear clean and contaminated areas in the dental surgery to prevent cross-contamination.
* **Supporting Instrument Reprocessing:** Carrying out or supporting point-of-use care, cleaning, packaging, and sterilisation tracking of reusable instruments.
* **Managing Clinical Waste and Sharps:** Segregating general waste, clinical or biohazard waste, and sharps waste safely at the point of generation.
* **Monitoring Environmental Controls:** Performing routine care and checks for dental unit waterlines, suction systems, and decontamination equipment.
* **Reporting Hazards and Incidents:** Identifying and reporting infection hazards, near-misses, equipment faults, or blood and body fluid exposure incidents.

Microbiology is the study of microscopic organisms. Understanding basic microbiology helps dental assistants appreciate why specific cleaning, disinfection, and sterilisation procedures are necessary.

## 1.5 Resident Microbiota, Pathogens, Colonisation, Infection, and Disease
The human oral cavity naturally harbors hundreds of species of microorganisms, known as resident microbiota (oral microbiome). In a healthy individual, these live on mucosal surfaces and teeth without causing harm. However, if introduced into normally sterile body sites during surgical procedures, resident microbiota can act as an opportunistic pathogen.

* **Pathogen:** A microorganism capable of causing infection or disease in a host.
* **Opportunistic Pathogen:** A microorganism that usually does not cause disease in a healthy host with intact defences, but can cause infection if immunity is compromised or tissue barriers are breached.

> **DISTINCT MICROBIAL STATES**
> * **COLONISATION:** Microorganisms establish and multiply on body surfaces (e.g. skin or mucosa) without invading tissues or causing damage or an immune response.
> * **INFECTION:** Microorganisms enter and multiply within host tissues. Infection can be asymptomatic (subclinical) or lead to clinical disease.
> * **DISEASE:** Clinical state where infection results in demonstrable tissue damage or physiological dysfunction, producing observable signs and symptoms (pain, inflammation, fever).

## 1.6 Contamination and Cross-Contamination
* **Contamination:** The presence of microorganisms or other unwanted material on a person, surface, instrument, item or environment.
* **Cross-Contamination:** The transfer of infectious agents from one source (such as a patient, contaminated surface, or instrument) to another person or object.

Pathways of cross-contamination include direct physical contact, indirect contact via contaminated intermediate objects (fomites), and droplet/aerosol dispersion generated during high-speed dental procedures.

## 1.7 Reservoirs and Portals of Entry/Exit
A **reservoir** is defined as any person, animal, plant, substance, or environment in which an infectious agent normally lives, grows, or multiplies. Key reservoirs include human hosts (patients and dental team members) and dental unit waterlines (DUWLs), where inner tubing walls develop microbial biofilm.

**Portals of Exit** include saliva, blood, respiratory droplets, and skin lesion exudate. **Portals of Entry** include non-intact skin (cuts, abrasions, percutaneous injuries), mucous membranes (eyes, nose, mouth), and open surgical sites.

## 1.8 Modes of Transmission
Infectious agents spread in healthcare settings via direct contact, indirect contact (fomites), respiratory droplets, dental aerosols generated by high-speed equipment, and percutaneous exposure (sharps injuries). Standard and Transmission-Based Precautions are designed to interrupt these pathways.

## 1.9 The Chain of Infection and Interruption Measures
Disease transmission depends on six continuous links. Interrupting one or more links stops transmission:
1. **Infectious Agent:** Pathogenic microorganisms. Interrupted by effective cleaning, disinfection, and steam sterilisation.
2. **Reservoir:** Where microorganisms survive. Interrupted by surface cleaning, DUWL maintenance, and clinical waste disposal.
3. **Portal of Exit:** Escape pathway from reservoir. Interrupted by rubber dam isolation and High-Volume Evacuation (HVE).
4. **Mode of Transmission:** Transmission pathway. Interrupted by hand hygiene, disposable barriers, and clinical zoning.
5. **Portal of Entry:** Entry pathway into host. Interrupted by protective eyewear, surgical masks, gloves, and ANTT.
6. **Susceptible Host:** Host with compromised barriers. Interrupted by staff immunisation (e.g. Hepatitis B) and health monitoring.

## 1.10 Workplace Infection Hazards and Risk Identification
Workplace health and safety requires identifying hazards and applying the Hierarchy of Controls to reduce risks. For the purposes of this guide, infection-related hazards are managed across SIX levels:

> **SAFE WORK AUSTRALIA HIERARCHY OF CONTROLS (6 LEVELS)**
> 1. **ELIMINATION:** Completely removing the hazard from the workplace (e.g. eliminating unnecessary manual tasks).
> 2. **SUBSTITUTION:** Replacing a hazard or process with a safer alternative.
> 3. **ISOLATION:** Separating the hazard from workers by physical barriers or distance (e.g. dedicated reprocessing room).
> 4. **ENGINEERING CONTROLS:** Physical equipment isolating workers from hazards (e.g. safety sharps, HVE, hands-free taps).
> 5. **ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROLS:** Work policies, protocols, clinical zoning rules, and training.
> 6. **PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE):** Personal barriers worn by workers (gloves, masks, eyewear, gowns).

## 1.11 Recording and Reporting Infection Risks and Incidents
Prompt identification, documentation, and reporting of infection risks, near-misses, and exposure incidents are essential for workplace safety. Types of incidents include percutaneous sharps injuries, mucocutaneous splashes, IPC protocol breaches, and equipment faults.

Immediate response for blood/body fluid exposure includes performing immediate first aid (washing percutaneous wounds gently with soap and water without scrubbing or forcibly squeezing), notifying the supervisor immediately, seeking urgent medical assessment, and completing factual workplace documentation.
