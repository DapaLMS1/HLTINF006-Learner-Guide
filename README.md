Unit Introduction & Welcome
Welcome to your Learner Guide for HLTINF006 Apply basic principles and practices of infection prevention and control. This unit describes the performance outcomes, skills, and knowledge required to evaluate and manage infection risks, apply standard and transmission-based precautions, and respond to infection control incidents within a healthcare setting.

In clinical dental practice, infection prevention and control (IPC) is fundamental to patient safety and worker health. As a dental assistant, you play a vital role in executing daily infection control protocols, maintaining clean and sterile environments, managing clinical waste and sharps, and protecting patients and the dental team from healthcare-associated infections.

Aim
This unit of competency describes the skills and knowledge required to apply basic infection prevention and control principles in work settings, including implementing standard and transmission-based precautions and responding to risks.

This unit applies to individuals working in a range of industry and work setting contexts who are required to follow established infection control guidelines to maintain a safe work environment in accordance with Commonwealth and State/Territory legislation, Australian/New Zealand Standards, and industry codes of practice.

What Will I Learn?
We are working with you to support you in developing your competence in the following areas:

Follow standard precautions for infection prevention and control

Implement transmission-based precautions

Respond to infection risks and management of exposures

Maintain clean work areas and equipment to minimise contamination risks

How Will I Be Assessed?
Your assessment will be completed directly into the aNewSpring Learning Management System (LMS). These assessments incorporate:

Online Quizzes: To test your foundational understanding of infection prevention and control principles and procedures.

Written Knowledge Questions: To demonstrate your depth of knowledge regarding infection control guidelines, hazard identification, transmission pathways, and risk management.

Online Practical Tasks: Including video submissions to demonstrate your practical skills (e.g., performing correct hand hygiene, donning and doffing PPE appropriately, and applying standard and transmission-based precautions) in a simulated or actual work environment.

Each assessment item must be assessed as 'Satisfactory'. The final assessment outcome will recognise you as 'Competent' once all items are confirmed satisfactory.

How to Use This Learner Guide
This Learner Guide is structured into three core modules followed by practical workplace quick-reference tools and templates:

Module 1 – Infection Prevention and Control Foundations: Covers IPC purpose, legal WHS governance, regulatory frameworks, core microbiology, reservoirs, modes of transmission, the Chain of Infection, and risk management.

Module 2 – Standard Precautions, Hand Hygiene, PPE, and Dental Environmental Controls: Covers standard precautions, WHO 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene, flexible PPE selection, respiratory etiquette, ANTT, clinical zoning, surface cleaning, disposable barriers, DUWL maintenance, suction systems, and linen management.

Module 3 – Hazards, Occupational Exposure, Waste, Spills, Incident Reporting and Basic Reprocessing: Covers hazard identification, occupational exposure response, sharps safety, clinical waste segregation, spill management, incident reporting, sterile storage, basic instrument reprocessing workflow, and clinical appointment integration.

Appendices 1–5: Practical quick-reference guides, decision-tree flowcharts, hazard logs, and incident reporting prompt sheets designed for clinical placement support.
