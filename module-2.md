# Module 2 – Standard Precautions, Hand Hygiene, PPE, and Dental Environmental Controls

## 2.1 Standard Precautions in Dental Practice
Standard Precautions are the fundamental work practices required to achieve a basic level of IPC across all healthcare settings. In dental practice, Standard Precautions must be applied during the care of every patient, regardless of their perceived or confirmed infection status.

It is impossible to reliably identify every patient carrying an infectious microorganism. Applying Standard Precautions universally ensures that IPC practices do not depend on knowing a patient's infection status or medical history, establishing a consistent baseline of safety across all clinical encounters.

## 2.2 Hand Hygiene
Hand hygiene is a primary measure for reducing microorganism transmission in healthcare settings. Hands are a common pathway for transferring microorganisms between patients, equipment, and surfaces.

The 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene in dental practice include: (1) Before touching a patient, (2) Before a clean/aseptic procedure, (3) After body fluid exposure risk, (4) After touching a patient, and (5) After touching patient surroundings. Hand hygiene must always be performed immediately before donning clinical gloves and immediately after glove removal.

* **Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (ABHR):** Preferred method for routine clinical hand hygiene when hands are visibly clean. Applied to dry hands and rubbed thoroughly until dry.
* **Liquid Soap and Water:** Required when hands are visibly soiled or contaminated with blood, body fluids, or organic matter. Dried using single-use paper towels.

**Hand Health:** Wrist jewellery and rings should be avoided as they harbor microorganisms. Natural fingernails must be kept short and clean. Intact skin is an essential natural barrier; cuts or abrasions must be covered with waterproof dressings.

## 2.3 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) provides a physical barrier between the worker and potential hazards. PPE represents the last level in the Hierarchy of Controls and must be used alongside higher-level engineering and administrative controls.

* **Clinical Gloves:** Protect hands from blood/saliva contact. Single-use only; changed between patients and dirty/clean tasks. Never washed or re-used.
* **Surgical Masks:** Protect nose and mouth from splashes and spray. Must cover nose and mouth correctly. Changed when damp, soiled, damaged, or between procedures.
* **Protective Eyewear / Face Shields:** Protect eyes and face from splatter. Prescription glasses require protective side shields. Reusable eyewear is cleaned/disinfected between uses.
* **Protective Clothing / Gowns:** Protect skin and work uniform. Selected based on anticipated fluid exposure.

**Safe Donning and Removal:** PPE should be put on and removed in a logical, controlled sequence following workplace procedures and guidance to avoid self-contamination.

## 2.4 Respiratory Hygiene and Cough Etiquette
Respiratory hygiene measures prevent the spread of respiratory secretions. Principles include covering coughs/sneezes with tissues or inner elbow, immediate tissue disposal into hands-free receptacles, performing hand hygiene, providing masks to symptomatic patients where appropriate, and managing symptomatic patients based on care urgency, public health guidance, and workplace policy.

## 2.5 Aseptic Non-Touch Technique (ANTT)
Aseptic technique describes clinical practices preventing microorganism transfer to susceptible host sites. Aseptic Non-Touch Technique (ANTT) is a standardized framework maintaining aseptic principles.

* **Key Sites:** Surgical wounds, extraction sockets, exposed pulp tissue, or mucosal incision sites where microorganisms could establish infection.
* **Key Parts:** Specific parts of sterile or clean equipment coming into direct contact with Key Sites or sterile materials (e.g. working tips of sterile burs, elevators, suture needles, root canal instruments).

**Core Principle:** Identified Key Parts and Key Sites must NOT be touched or contaminated during an aseptic procedure.

## 2.6 Clinical Zoning and Clean/Contaminated Workflow
Physical work areas in the dental surgery are organized into clinical zones to maintain clear separation:
* **Clean Area:** Designated for preparing and storing clean or sterile instruments and materials away from splash or contamination.
* **Contaminated Area:** Designated for clinical treatment, placing contaminated instruments, and managing used items.

Pre-procedure preparation involves dispensing required materials before care begins. Contaminated instruments are transported in covered, rigid, puncture-resistant containers to prevent environmental contamination.

## 2.7 Environmental Cleaning and Surface Management
Environmental surfaces are classified into clinical contact surfaces (frequently touched, requiring cleaning and/or disinfection between patients or barrier protection) and general environmental/housekeeping surfaces (floors, walls, maintained via routine cleaning with water and neutral detergent).

**Cleaning before Disinfection:** Visible organic bioburden or soil must be removed by physical cleaning before chemical disinfection can be effective. Avoid spraying liquids directly onto electronic equipment or dental units.

## 2.8 Barriers and Disposable Surface Covers
Disposable physical barriers protect clinical contact surfaces that are difficult to clean or contain sensitive electronics (e.g. light handles, 3-way syringe bodies, touchscreens). Barriers are removed carefully with gloves between patients and disposed of per workplace waste procedures. Barriers complement but do not replace routine surface cleaning.

## 2.9 Dental Unit Waterlines (DUWLs)
Dental unit waterlines consist of narrow plastic tubing susceptible to microbial biofilm development on inner walls. Dental assistants perform waterline flushing, chemical dosing, and maintenance strictly according to manufacturer instructions and workplace procedures. For surgical procedures involving open bone or mucosal reflection, standard DUWL water must not be used; sterile water or sterile saline delivered via independent sterile delivery systems must be used.

## 2.10 Dental Suction and Evacuation Systems
Suction systems remove fluids and assist in aerosol control. Saliva ejector patients should not close their lips tightly around low-volume tips to prevent potential backflow. High-Volume Evacuation (HVE) is an important aerosol control measure used during aerosol-generating procedures where indicated. Single-use suction components are discarded after use.

## 2.11 Linen, Uniforms, and Protective Clothing
Contaminated linen or clothing must be handled gently with minimal shaking to reduce the risk of dispersing contamination. Soiled items are placed in designated laundry containers. Workplaces maintain policies regarding clinical uniform changing, transport, and laundering.

## 2.12 Cleaning Equipment and Reusable Environmental Items
Cleaning tools must be appropriate for intended tasks, separated between clinical and non-clinical areas, cleaned, dried thoroughly, and stored neatly. Cleaning chemicals must be clearly labeled with Safety Data Sheets (SDS) accessible.
